Why Listen

Not a generic pitch. The actual reason.

I first heard about Listen from the SF billboard — an algorithmic bouncer for Berghain, no logo, no explanation. That's a very specific piece of brand instinct: make people work to get in, and the ones who solve it are exactly who you wanted in the room. I've spent my career building the same kind of pull in reverse — a venture fund people now compete to join, a bank's flagship sales conference people want a seat at. Communities people want to be let into, not sold on.

What actually convinced me is the thesis underneath the product: a survey is just a proxy for the real thing, which is a conversation. I don't run programs — I run conversations, then turn what I hear into methodology, curriculum, and trust. That's what "unreasonable hospitality" has meant in my work: not being nice, but listening first.

I've spent six years being the person companies bring in specifically to go talk to their people — senior leaders, sales teams, survey respondents by the thousand — and come back with something true. I'd like to be the person who does that for Listen's own customers next.
